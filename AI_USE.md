# Use of artificial intelligence

NASA Space Apps asks teams to say how they used AI. This is the full account. The site repeats the short version in its Credits dialog, so it is visible without opening the repository.

## Tools

- **Claude (Anthropic), in Claude Code.** Used for most of the work listed below.
- **Devin (Cognition).** Used by Juan Ignacio Angeloni, from the team, in his own version of the project (github.com/JuanIAngeloni/still-there). From that version this site takes the selection of NASA photos with their captions, two audio clips and the choice of the Viking 1 and Ingenuity models.

No image, video, audio or 3D model in the site was generated by AI. Photos, audio, video, maps and the official 3D models are NASA's. The models that are not NASA's were written as code (see below).

## What AI did

- **Code.** It wrote most of the site's code (Earth scene, start page, 3D launch scene, journey through space, surfaces, exploration mode) and the tools in `tools/`, following the team's design decisions. Each version was checked in the browser.
- **Finding and checking data.** It searched and compared official sources (NSSDCA, NASA Science, JPL, LROC, HiRISE, press kits, flight reports) for dates, coordinates, distances, last contacts, launch sequences and the fate of every rocket stage. One pass searched and a second, independent pass tried to refute each fact. Every fact has its link in `SOURCES.md` or next to it in the site. Disagreements between sources and facts we could not confirm are written down in `docs/etapas/*.md` and `docs/verificacion_ciencia.md`.
- **Trajectories.** It wrote the script that downloads JPL Horizons tables. The trajectories shown are Horizons data, not invented, except the ones marked as reconstructed.
- **Texts.** It drafted the texts for children in Spanish and English from that data, including the questions, the "what we learned" cards and the first-person lines on the start page. Those first-person lines are not real quotes; the site says so.
- **Models written as code.** The simplified models of Surveyor 1, Phoenix, Mars Pathfinder and Sojourner, the Mars cruise capsule, the Apollo 11 surface experiments, and the Saturn V, Titan IIIE-Centaur and Atlas-Centaur rockets were programmed by AI from published dimensions. They are not NASA models and carry no NASA emblems.
- **Adapting NASA models.** It wrote the code that splits NASA's Delta II and Atlas V models into separable parts, repeats the Delta II booster to reach nine, removes one booster from the Atlas V 551 and places InSight's instruments on the ground. Each change is listed in `SOURCES.md`.

## What the team did

- The idea, the concept, the choice of the fourteen machines and every design decision.
- Reviewing each version, rejecting what did not work and asking for changes: the continuous zoom, the solar system as the starting view, real launch videos, the start page layout, the 3D launch with the fate of each stage, and the push for realistic rockets and spacecraft.
- Choosing the NASA photos, captions and audio (Juan Ignacio Angeloni).
- Approving every download of NASA material and checking the result on screen.
